# Field Operations Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure governs the conduct of all deployed security
personnel across guarding assignments. It applies to static guarding, mobile
patrol, and concierge postings. It is read alongside the [[guard-handbook]] for
conduct and employment terms, the [[incident-response-matrix]] for severity
grading, and the site-specific [[post-orders-bluebay]] where a posting has its
own instructions. Where a post order and this SOP conflict, the post order
prevails for that site only.

## 2. Shift Start Procedure

On arrival, the officer signs the attendance register, collects the radio and
keys from the outgoing officer, and completes a handover note. The officer
verifies that the panic alarm, CCTV recorder, and access-control terminal are
operational before the outgoing officer departs. Any equipment fault is logged
in the Daily Occurrence Book and reported to the control room immediately.

## 3. Patrol and Check-Call Cadence

During a day shift, officers conduct a perimeter patrol once per hour. During a
night shift, the cadence increases: officers must make a radio check-call to the
control room every 30 minutes, and any missed check-call triggers a welfare
escalation after two consecutive misses. Patrol routes must vary so that timings
are not predictable. Each patrol is logged with a timestamp and the officer's
collar number.

## 4. Visitor and Access Management

Visitors are signed in at the gatehouse, issued a temporary pass, and escorted
where the post order requires it. Contractors must present a works order. Deliveries
are checked against the expected-deliveries sheet. Officers do not admit anyone
whose identity cannot be verified, and refusals are logged and reported.

## 5. Use of Force and Conduct

Officers act lawfully and proportionately at all times. Physical intervention is
a last resort, used only to prevent imminent harm, and every instance is reported
the same shift. Conduct standards, including grooming and uniform, are set out in
the [[guard-handbook]] and are not repeated here.

## 6. Incident Grading and Escalation

Incidents are graded against the [[incident-response-matrix]]. The grade sets the
escalation path and the speed of response. The grades are Code Green (routine,
log only), Code Amber (significant, notify duty manager within the shift), and
Code Red (critical — threat to life, major loss, or armed intrusion).

### 6.1 Code Red Escalation Timeline

A Code Red is the most serious classification and has a strict timeline that every
officer must commit to memory. On identifying a Code Red event, the officer must:
call 999 immediately and only then the control room; within 2 minutes, the control
room raises the on-call Operations Manager; within 5 minutes, the Operations
Manager notifies the client's nominated emergency contact and the Operations
Director; within 15 minutes, a written flash report is issued to the client. The
scene is preserved for police and nothing is touched. A full incident review is
convened within 24 hours. Failure to meet the Code Red timeline is treated as a
gross-conduct matter under the [[guard-handbook]].

## 7. End of Shift

The officer completes the Daily Occurrence Book, hands over outstanding matters,
returns equipment, and signs out. Nothing in this section overrides the handover
verification in Section 2.
